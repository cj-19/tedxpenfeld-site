The project

Why a day of ideas in Brest, what it brings to the region, and what it is not.

What was missing

Brest has everything a conference of ideas needs: a university, engineering schools, dense marine and digital research, two ports, a naval base, a sailing industry, an active cultural scene, a broad network of associations.

What was missing was the format that brings them together.

There was no general, demanding, public stage here where a marine biologist, an artist, a sailor, a nurse and an engineer speak on the same day, to the same audience, in the same short format — without that audience having come for any one of them.

None of the existing formats manufactures the unlikely encounter. TEDxPenfeld exists to do exactly that.

What it brings

A stage for voices that don't have one. A TEDx event is not a platform for professional speakers. Its purpose is to give the floor to people who rarely get it: the doctoral student who has only ever presented to her jury, the head of a small company who solved a problem no one documented, the public servant who saw something their organisation has not yet seen.

Work on the idea, not just a microphone. Every speaker is coached over several months to build their talk. That work is what separates a TEDx talk from an ordinary presentation.

A lasting record. Talks are recorded and published free on the global TEDx channel. A local conference stops at the door of the room; a published talk starts when it goes online.

A meeting point between worlds that coexist without speaking. In the room, no one wears a job title. On stage, no one represents their organisation — each person defends an idea.

What it is not

It is not a sector conference. It is not a sales platform: no speaker promotes a product, a book or a company. It is not a platform for political, religious or commercial interests. It is not a fundraiser — the event cannot generate profit.

And it is not TED. TEDxPenfeld is an independently organised event, operated under license from TED. We do not speak on behalf of TED.

One hundred seats

The capacity is capped by our license. We treat it as a feature: with one hundred people, the welcome is personal, the breaks are usable, and conversations actually happen.

There will be no difference of status in the room — no reserved section, no privileged category. Everyone attends the same day, under the same conditions.

Who runs it

An entirely volunteer team, within Penfeld Conférences, an independent French non-profit association. No one is paid — neither the team nor the speakers. That is a TEDx rule.

This independent TEDx event is operated under license from TED.
